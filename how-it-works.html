A trip has four stages. Waypost is built around all four

Most planning tools help with the middle and abandon you at the airport. This is what each stage looks like inside Waypost, and what it costs you in effort.

Stage one

Say what kind of trip this is

Dates or a rough window, a budget band per person, who is coming, and how hard you want to push each day. Waypost asks five questions and nothing more.

Takes about two minutes. You can change any answer later without losing the plan built on it.

Stage two

Get a draft route, not a blank page

Waypost lays out the days: where you sleep, how long each transfer really takes, and which afternoons have nothing scheduled on purpose.

Every leg carries a travel-time estimate with a buffer, so a delayed train does not silently break the following three days.

Stage three

Settle it with the people coming

Invite your travel party. Everyone can move a stop, flag a cost or open a question, and every change shows what it did to the rest of the trip.

Open questions sit on the day they affect until somebody closes them. Nothing important stays buried in a chat thread.

Stage four

Carry it, offline, on the road

Download the trip before you fly. Day cards, directions, stored tickets and addresses in the local script all work without a connection.

Edits you make with no signal sync the next time you are online, and everyone in the party sees them.

What is doing the work underneath

None of this is magic. It is a small number of things done carefully.

  • Real transfer times, not straight lines

    Waypost uses scheduled public transport and typical road times between your stops, then adds a buffer sized to the leg. A 40-minute city hop and a 6-hour mountain drive do not get the same padding.

  • Closures and opening hours built in

    Museums shut on Mondays, temples close early, and some passes only run in summer. Anything you add carries its own hours, and the day flags the conflict before you commit to it.

  • Decisions with a record

    Every swap, vote and comment stays attached to the day it changed. Six weeks later, nobody has to remember why the Florence night got cut.

  • Costs that stay current

    Add a stay or a train and the per-person total moves immediately, in your currency, with the split rule your group actually uses.

The part that matters once you have landed

Planning tools are judged in the two weeks you are away, not the six weeks you spent preparing. Waypost is designed for a phone with 14% battery and no roaming.

  • One tap to take the whole trip offline Day cards, addresses, directions and stored tickets, all available with no connection.
  • Addresses in the local script Show the driver the address the way the driver reads it, not a transliteration.
  • Reminders that respect the time zone you are in Not the one you booked from, and not the one your phone thinks you are in.

Practical questions about using it

How long does a first plan take to build?

The initial draft appears within a minute of you answering the five setup questions. Getting it to a plan a group is happy with usually takes a few evenings of edits, which is the part Waypost is really built to shorten.

Can I import bookings I have already made?

Yes. Forward a confirmation email to your trip address and Waypost reads the dates, times and reference, then drops it on the right day. You can also add anything by hand.

Does everyone in my group need to pay?

No. One subscription covers the trip. Everyone you invite can view, edit and comment on that trip at no cost to them. They only need their own plan if they want to organize trips of their own.

Which devices does it run on?

Any modern browser on desktop, tablet or phone, plus the iOS and Android apps. Offline mode works on the mobile apps and on browsers that support offline storage.
