We build the boring half of travel so the good half survives

Waypost is eleven people in Denver and four time zones, making planning software for travelers who would rather be walking than reconciling a spreadsheet.

It started with a trip that fell apart on day two

In 2021, six of us planned two weeks across Patagonia on a shared document, three chat groups and a folder of screenshots. A single road closure took four hours to work around, because nobody could see what else the day was carrying.

The problem was never a shortage of information. It was that no two pieces of it lived in the same place, and none of it knew about the rest. We built the first version of Waypost for our own next trip, then for the friends who kept asking to borrow it.

Five years later the company is still small, still funded by subscriptions rather than commissions, and still built by people who use it on their own holidays.

How the company got here

  1. 2021

    Built for one trip

    The first version was a private tool for a group of six planning two weeks across Patagonia. It did one thing: keep the day plan and the travel times in the same view.

  2. 2022

    Opened to anyone

    Waypost Travel Technologies was incorporated in Delaware, the Denver office opened, and the planner went public with a free tier. Group editing arrived that October.

  3. 2023

    Offline mode

    The most requested feature by a distance. Day cards, directions and tickets became available with no connection, which is when people started using it during the trip and not only before it.

  4. 2024

    Shared budgets

    Cost splitting landed with the rules groups actually use, and the destination library grew to sixteen places we could plan properly rather than a hundred we could only list.

  5. 2026

    Still subscription funded

    No booking commissions, no advertising inside the product, no reselling of travel data. The people who pay us are the people using the software.

Three commitments we hold ourselves to

These are the ones that cost us money, which is how you know they are real.

No commissions, ever

We take nothing from the hotels, airlines or operators you choose. That is why our suggestions can be inconvenient for them and useful for you.

Your trips are not a product

We do not sell personal data, and we do not use trip contents for advertising. Read the specifics in our privacy policy rather than taking our word for it.

Honest limits, stated up front

Estimates are labeled as estimates. When we do not know something, the product says so instead of guessing confidently.
